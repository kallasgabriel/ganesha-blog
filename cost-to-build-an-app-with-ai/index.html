How Much Does It Cost to Build an App with AI?

What it really costs to build and host an app with AI when you're not a developer: a $0 setup, a real monthly bill, and the charges that surprise you.

If you are not a developer, you can build an app with AI and keep it online for $0 a month while you are learning, and expect roughly $20–$70 a month once it has real users. The expensive part is the building, not the running: the AI tool that writes your app is usually the biggest line on the bill, and it is the one you can stop paying for once the app works.

This article breaks down what you are actually paying for, with three realistic scenarios and one worked example you can copy.

What are you actually paying for?

Almost every "I built an app with AI" bill is made of four things:

  • The AI builder — the tool that writes and edits the app for you (Lovable, Bolt, Replit, v0, or a chat assistant like ChatGPT or Claude). Usually a monthly subscription, often metered in credits.
  • Hosting — where the app lives so other people can open it (Vercel, Netlify, Cloudflare Pages, Render). Free for small personal projects.
  • A database — only if your app needs to remember things between visits, for more than one person (Supabase, Neon, Firebase). Free for small projects.
  • A domain name — optional. A yearly fee from a registrar. Check the renewal price, not the first-year promo price.

Notice what is not on the list: you do not pay per user, and you do not pay a developer. That is the whole point of this route.

Scenario 1: you are learning and nobody uses it yet — $0/month

This is where most people should start, and it genuinely costs nothing.

  • AI builder: free tier. Lovable's free plan is $0 with 5 credits a day — enough for a few meaningful prompts per day, which is about the right pace when you are learning anyway.
  • Hosting: free. Vercel's Hobby plan is free, but it is restricted to personal, non-commercial projects.
  • Database: free. Supabase's free plan gives you a 500 MB database and 5 GB of egress — far more than a practice project will ever use.
  • Domain: skip it. Your host gives you a free address like my-app.vercel.app.

Total: $0. If someone tells you that you need to spend money to learn this, they are selling something.

Scenario 2: a small real app with a few real users

Now you want the app finished faster and shared with people. Two things change.

First, you hit the credit ceiling. Free-tier credits run out quickly once you start fixing details, because every correction costs a credit too. Lovable's paid plans start at $25/month for 100 credits, and that is the realistic cost of finishing something.

Second, the free hosting tier may no longer apply. A personal recipe tracker is fine on a free plan. An app for your shop, your clients, or your team is not personal use — Vercel Pro is $20 per seat per month.

Typical range: $25–$45/month while you are actively building.

Scenario 3: the app is part of how you make money

Add a paid database once you outgrow the free one: Supabase Pro starts at $25/month. Note the word starts — it is a base fee, not a flat rate, with compute, extra storage and extra egress billed on top.

Typical range: $45–$95/month, plus usage. At this point you are running infrastructure, and the useful question stops being "what does it cost?" and becomes "does the app earn or save more than that?"

A worked example: a task app for a 12-person team

Here is a real shape of a bill, month by month.

Month 1 — building it

  • Lovable Pro: $25 (you will use most of the 100 credits on corrections)
  • Vercel Pro: $20 (it is a work tool, not a personal project)
  • Supabase Free: $0 (12 people writing text tasks is nowhere near 500 MB)
  • Domain: one yearly fee from your registrar

Total: $45

Month 2 onwards — just running it

  • Lovable: cancelled — the app is done; you only resubscribe for a month when you want changes
  • Vercel Pro: $20
  • Supabase Free: $0

Total: $20/month

That drop from $45 to $20 is the single most useful thing to understand about this whole model. The AI builder is a project cost, not a running cost. Treat it like renting a tool: subscribe, build in focused sessions, ship, cancel. Plan your work in batches so you are not paying $25 a month to make one button blue.

Three charges that surprise beginners

1. Debugging burns credits. The prompt that builds the feature is cheap. The six prompts that fix it are not. Write clearer requests up front and you cut your bill roughly in half — this is a skill, not luck.

2. "Free" hosting often means "non-commercial". The moment money changes hands, read the plan terms. Finding out later is worse than paying $20 now.

3. Base fees are not total fees. Database and hosting plans advertise a starting price and bill usage on top. Before you launch anything public, set a spending limit or billing alert in the dashboard. A badly configured app that loops in the background can run up usage while you sleep.

How to keep the bill near zero while you learn

  • Build on free tiers until something actually works. Do not buy anything on day one.
  • Use the free host's default address instead of a domain until a real person needs to visit.
  • Add a database only when your app must remember data for more than one person. Browser-local storage is free and enough for a personal tracker.
  • Subscribe to the AI builder for one focused month, finish the project, then cancel.
  • Keep a backup of your project files. Re-creating lost work is the most expensive mistake on this list.

The cost question usually hides a different question: can I actually do this? The money is not the obstacle — a working first project costs nothing. What takes practice is describing what you want clearly, checking what the AI produced, and guiding the corrections. Frequently asked questions

Can I really build and host an app with AI for free?

Yes, for a personal project. AI builders, hosting and databases all have free tiers that are enough to build something real and put it online — for example, Supabase's free plan includes a 500 MB database. You only start paying when you need more capacity or when the project is commercial.

Why is my AI builder bill higher than the advertised plan price?

Because these tools bill in credits, and corrections consume credits like new features do. Lovable Pro starts at $25/month for 100 credits, but a long debugging session can push you into top-ups. Clearer initial requests are the cheapest fix.

Do I have to pay for hosting if the app is for my business?

Usually yes. Vercel's free Hobby plan is restricted to personal, non-commercial projects, while Pro is $20 per seat per month. Other hosts have similar clauses, so check the terms before you launch anything that makes money.

What is the cheapest way to keep a finished app online?

Cancel the AI builder subscription once the app works and keep only hosting, plus a database if you need one. For a small team tool that is often around $20 a month, and $0 if the project qualifies for a free hosting tier.

Do I need a database at all?

Only if the app must remember data across devices or across users. A personal task list or notes app can store everything in your browser for free. Add a database when two people need to see the same data.
